ok now lets review the vision against the writing guidelines below. they're similar to /writing-refinement : 

1. Structure via the "S-C-Q" Framework
A vision statement is most engaging when it resolves a specific tension
. Barbara Minto suggests using a narrative flow to establish relevance before presenting your "Answer" (the vision)
.
Situation: Begin with a non-controversial statement about the current state of your field or product area that your audience already knows or will accept as true
.
Complication: Describe what has changed or what problem has arisen in that situation (e.g., a technological shift, a lagging performance, or an unmet customer need)
.
Question: This complication should naturally trigger a question in the reader's mind (e.g., "How can we adapt?" or "What should our strategy be?")
.
Answer (The Vision): Your vision statement serves as the resolution to this story
.
2. Motivate through "Practical Problems"
A compelling vision must address a problem your audience actually cares about
.
Define the Cost: Joseph Williams and Joseph Bizup emphasize that a problem is only a problem if it has a cost—a palpable consequence like lost time, money, or happiness
. Your vision is the "solution" to this cost
.
Focus on Themes: Put your most important concepts in the "stress position" (at the end) of your introductory sentences to signal the key themes you will develop
.
3. Infuse Humanity and Warmth
William Zinsser argues that "clutter" and "creeping nounism" are the diseases of business writing
.
Avoid "Corporate Robots": Do not use abstractions like "profitability" or "implementation"
. Instead, use active verbs and real nouns that allow the reader to visualize someone doing something
.
Locate the "I": A vision statement should reflect the identity and enthusiasm of the person behind it
. Zinsser suggests writing the first draft in the first person ("I" or "we") to keep the tone warm, even if you remove the pronouns later
.
Be Natural: Never say anything in writing that you wouldn't comfortably say in conversation
.
4. Apply the Principle of "Thinking Small"
To make a large vision engaging, don't try to be definitive or all-encompassing, which can lead to "unwieldy writing" that drains enthusiasm
.
Find the "Window": Like a memoir, a good vision statement shouldn't be the summary of an entire life or company; it should be a window into the intended future
.
One Provocative Thought: Every successful piece of nonfiction should leave the reader with exactly one provocative thought they didn't have before
.
5. Practice Ruthless Concision
A strong vision statement must be "stripped to its cleanest components"
.
Prune Redundancy: Eliminate "meaningless words" (like actually, basically, particular) and "redundant pairs" (like full and complete)
.
End with Strength: Your final sentence should encapsulate the vision and conclude with a jolt of fitness or surprise
.
